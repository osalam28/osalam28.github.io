How I Work

Four things I won't
negotiate on.

Not values-slide platitudes. The operating decisions that separate teams who ship features from teams who move metrics.

01 — Decision Systems

Clear decision rights protect the team

Prioritization rules and lightweight governance where it matters — so work aligns to outcomes and tradeoffs happen early, not in sprint review or post-launch.

02 — Discovery

Discovery has to earn the build

Problem clarity, a testable value hypothesis, and experiments that reduce rework downstream — before a single line of code is written.

03 — Measurement

Measurement isn't a phase — it's the work

Feedback loops built into every delivery so teams learn fast and can prove what changed because of what they shipped — not just what they shipped.

04 — AI

AI applied to real problems, not hype cycles

Recommendations, personalization, support automation — all measured, governed, and built with appropriate human oversight. I hold a U.S. patent in applied ML.

About

The longer version.

I'm a digital product executive with 15+ years building at the intersection of customer experience, technology, and commercial outcomes. I've led product in QSR, retail commerce, financial services, and enterprise utility — which means I've had to make product work in very different organizational contexts, with very different constraints.

I led the 0→1 launch of Taco Bell Delivery — one of the first large-scale QSR delivery products — as one of a handful of PMs at a time when digital product wasn't yet a formal discipline there. That channel later became a $1B revenue stream. Before that, at WageWorks, I launched the nation's first public healthcare marketplace, supporting 10M+ users in a complex regulatory environment.

Since then I've led AI and innovation at Under Armour (securing $20M in board-approved capital), personalization and digital modernization at Capital Group, and now lead all digital — web, mobile, voice, and conversational AI — at Southern California Edison.

I care most about building the conditions for great product work: clear strategy, strong operating models, and teams that know what winning looks like before they start building.
